I enjoy teaching physics through a mix of intuition, discussion, and hands-on problem solving. In class, we move back and forth between the underlying ideas and the tools used to explore them—from pencil-and-paper calculations to Python and Julia notebooks.

## Previous courses

<div class="card shadow-sm border-0 mb-4">
  <div class="card-body p-4">
    <p class="small text-muted text-uppercase mb-1" style="letter-spacing: 0.04em;">Winter 2024–25 · University of Konstanz</p>
    <h3 class="card-title mt-0">Modelling Quantum Hardware: Open Dynamics and Control</h3>
    <p>How do we describe and control real quantum devices? We looked at platforms ranging from trapped ions and atomic clouds to photons and superconducting circuits, always keeping decoherence and experimental constraints in view.</p>
    <p class="mb-0"><strong>Along the way:</strong> cooling, squeezing, sensing, quantum simulation, many-body interactions, error correction, and nonlinear resonators—with practical work in Python and Julia.</p>

<div class="card shadow-sm border-0 mb-4">

<div class="card shadow-sm border-0 mb-4">

    <p>Quantum oscillators are a wonderfully compact setting for learning about driving, amplification, cooling, coupling, sensing, and control.</p>
